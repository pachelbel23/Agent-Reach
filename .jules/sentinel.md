## 2026-11-05 - SSRF Bypass via Embedded IPv4 in IPv6 Address Literals
**Vulnerability:** `normalize_public_http_url` relied on Python's `ipaddress` module and checked `literal_address.is_global`. In Python, `IPv6Address.is_global` returns `True` for IPv4-compatible (`::a.b.c.d`), IPv4-translated (`::ffff:0:a.b.c.d`), and NAT64 (`64:ff9b::a.b.c.d`) addresses even when the embedded IPv4 address is loopback, private, or link-local (e.g. `127.0.0.1`, `10.0.0.1`, or `169.254.169.254`), allowing SSRF protections to be bypassed.
**Learning:** Python's standard `IPv6Address.is_global` property does not recursively inspect embedded IPv4 representations inside IPv6 prefixes. Relying solely on `addr.is_global` leaves applications vulnerable to IPv6-framed private destination SSRF. Furthermore, `addr._ip` is not declared on `IPv6Address` in typeshed, so `int(addr)` should be used to extract the 128-bit integer representation safely without mypy typing errors.
**Prevention:** Always inspect IPv6 addresses for embedded IPv4 prefixes (IPv4-compatible `::/96`, IPv4-translated `::ffff:0:0/96`, and well-known NAT64 `64:ff9b::/96`) and extract the embedded IPv4 address to evaluate its `is_global` status alongside the outer address.
